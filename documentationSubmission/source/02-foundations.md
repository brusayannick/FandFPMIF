# Conceptual Foundations

## Process Mining: Terminology and Data Basis

Process mining sits between data science and process science and analyses event data to discover, monitor and improve real processes (van der Aalst, 2016). Rather than relying on how a process is assumed to run, it derives knowledge from the digital traces executions leave in an organisation's information systems, complementing the model-driven perspective of classical business process management (van der Aalst et al., 2012).

The starting point is the **event log**: a collection of **cases**, each a **trace** of **events** ordered in time. An event minimally records a **case identifier**, an **activity** label and a **timestamp**, often enriched with a **resource** or a completion timestamp (Reinkemeyer, 2020b). Traces sharing an activity sequence form a **variant**, and the variant distribution is usually the first thing an analyst inspects, because it separates dominant paths from the long tail of exceptions.

The **XES** standard (IEEE Standards Association, 2016) gives this an interchange format: XML with a fixed log-trace-event hierarchy and an extension mechanism assigning semantics to attribute keys. Tabular **CSV** carries the same information without the semantics; which column is the case identifier is recorded nowhere in the file and must be supplied at import, which is why the platform's CSV path needs a column-mapping step and the XES path does not (§4.6). **Object-centric process mining** relaxes the assumption that each event belongs to exactly one case (van der Aalst, 2019): in many processes several object types interact, and forcing such data into a single case notion produces the known distortions of convergence and divergence. The **OCEL** standard (Ghahfarokhi et al., 2021) represents these logs as events, objects and their relations.

One structural assumption underlies everything below. An event log is an **ordered, case-attributed sequence of recorded events, written once and read many times**, and the overwhelming majority of reads are aggregations over a few columns across many rows. Every later decision about storage and querying follows from it; §4.6 makes the derivation explicit.

## The Analytical Disciplines

The literature distinguishes three analysis types (van der Aalst, 2016; van der Aalst et al., 2012). **Process discovery** derives a model from a log with no a priori model; since several models can explain one log, model quality is weighed along four competing dimensions, **fitness** (does the model permit the log's behaviour), **precision** (does it permit little else), **generalisation** (does it permit plausible unseen behaviour) and **simplicity** (is it readable), which trade off, and which is why a platform must offer several discovery algorithms rather than one canonical choice. **Conformance checking** compares a model with a log to detect and quantify deviations (Rozinat & van der Aalst, 2008), producing fitness and precision scores and a localisation of the disagreement; replay-based and alignment-based techniques differ sharply in cost and informativeness (Carmona et al., 2022). **Enhancement** repairs or extends a model using the log, for instance by projecting bottlenecks onto it.

Read as a set, the three share a shape that matters architecturally. Each consumes a log, optionally consumes an artefact another technique produced, and produces an artefact. None needs to know where the log came from, who owns it, or how the result is displayed. This licenses the platform's central abstraction, **log in, artefact out**, and it is why that abstraction can be one uniform module contract rather than three plug-in types (§5.2).

Two further concerns motivate several bundled modules. **Concept drift** is a process changing while it is analysed (Bose et al., 2014), which undermines methods assuming a stable process: two iterations analysed as one log yield a model describing neither. The literature distinguishes sudden, gradual, incremental and recurring drift, and three tasks: change point detection, change localisation and characterisation, and change process discovery. Surveys agree no single method dominates on accuracy, latency, robustness and scalability at once (Adams et al., 2023; Elkhawaga et al., 2020; Sato et al., 2022), which is itself an argument for a platform on which several can be installed side by side. **Process complexity** describes how difficult a process is to understand, analyse or explain; structural measures computed over a model describe the intended design, while behavioural measures computed from the log capture what happened and correlate with the quality of models discovery produces from it (Augusto et al., 2022; Langer, 2025).

## Model Notations and Performance Measures

The platform renders models in five notations; formal semantics are out of scope. A **directly-follows graph** (DFG) has one node per activity and an edge from *a* to *b* whenever *b* directly followed *a*, annotated with frequency or mean duration; it is the most readable and the least expressive, since it cannot distinguish concurrency from interleaving. A **Petri net** of places and transitions does express concurrency, choice and synchronisation precisely, and the Alpha and Inductive miners produce one. A **process tree** composes sub-trees with sequence, choice, parallel and loop operators and is sound by construction, which is what the Inductive miner builds internally. A **heuristics net** sits between the DFG and the Petri net, suppressing infrequent behaviour by threshold before inferring dependencies. **BPMN** is the notation practitioners model in, and the platform converts discovered models into it so a discovered process can be compared with a documented one.

The time measures used are equally few. **Throughput** is cases completed per unit time and **lead time** the elapsed time from a case's first to last event. **Sojourn time** is the time an activity occupies and **waiting time** the interval between one activity completing and the next starting; separating the two needs a start and an end timestamp per event, which is why several modules declare `end_timestamp` as an optional column that unlocks extra output. Because these distributions are heavily right-skewed, **percentiles** rather than means are the honest summary, and the platform reports the 90th percentile alongside the mean.

## Platforms versus Libraries: A Conceptual Distinction

Chapter 3 compares a cloud suite, a desktop research framework and a Python package. Comparing them at all requires a criterion saying what they share and where they differ.

A process mining **library** provides algorithms: functions taking data structures and returning data structures, making no claim about where the data came from, who may see it, or what happens when a call takes ten minutes. PM4Py is the canonical example (Berti et al., 2023).

A process mining **platform** provides algorithms *and* what makes running them repeatedly, by several people, over time, tractable. It adds five things a library has not: **persistence**, so a log imported today is analysable tomorrow; **identity**, so there is a notion of who is asking and the answer differs by asker; **concurrency**, so several analyses and several people proceed without interfering; **an interface**, so results reach somebody who did not write the algorithm; and **a lifecycle for third-party extensions**, so capability can be added, configured, updated and removed by someone other than the original authors while the system is in use.

These five are the evaluation grid of Chapter 3, alongside criteria specific to this project's setting. The distinction is operational rather than evaluative: it does not make libraries inferior, but it says that turning a library into something an analyst can use is real work, and that measuring a tool against criteria it never intended to satisfy would be a category error. It also frames this project's contribution precisely. Mate implements very few process mining algorithms itself; almost all its analytical capability comes from PM4Py and from reference implementations published alongside papers. What it contributes is the platform layer, and specifically the fifth property, the extension lifecycle, which the existing tools treat least seriously.
